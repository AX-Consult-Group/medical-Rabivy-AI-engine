# Chen et al. (2025) - Kidney and Cardiovascular Outcomes Among Patients With CKD Receiving GLP-1 Receptor Agonists


## Chen et al. (2025): Untitled section


### p1

C hronic kidney disease (CKD), especially due to dia- betic kidney disease, represents a significant global burden. [1][2][3] Despite intensive glycemic control, adverse kidney complications have not been significantly reduced in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM). 4 Standard treatments, including blood pressure control and renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, offer only modest effectiveness in slowing CKD progression to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD). 5,6 Furthermore, CKD exacerbates cardiovascular (CV) risk in DM patients. 7 Though therapies like sodium/glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, and Finerenone have shown kidney and CV benefits, [8][9][10][11][12] residual risks of CKD progression and CV events persist, 13 underscoring unmet treatment needs. 14 Previous studies suggested that GLP-1 receptor agonists may offer CV and kidney protection in diabetic patients. 15 However, the effectiveness of these agents in individuals with a pre-existing reduced estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) remains unclear. First, as illustrated in Figure S1, previous randomized controlled cardiovascular outcome trials (CVOTs) mainly recruited patients with preserved eGFR or those classified in the low to moderately increased risk groups, according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) categorization. 16 Subsequent subgroup and post hoc analyses revealed that the benefits for patients with reduced baseline eGFR or those in the KDIGO high or very-high risk categories did not achieve statistical significance, 15,17,18 possibly due to insufficient sample sizes within these subgroups. Second, the main kidney protective benefits observed with GLP-1 receptor agonists were primarily driven by the prevention or reduction of the severity of macroalbuminuria. 15 However, the effects on delaying the decline in eGFR or reducing the incidence of kidney failure remain controversial. 8,[19][20][21][22][23] Third, the biological mechanisms underlying kidney protection via GLP-1 receptor agonists are also disputed, [24][25][26] prompting further investigation into their effectiveness in patients with impaired kidney vascular autoregulation and diminished glomerular and tubular function. 27 To address these uncertainties, our study investigated the kidney and CV protective effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists in patients with reduced baseline eGFR. With the release of new data from the first kidney outcomes trial FLOW, 28 which included approximately 80% of patients with a baseline eGFR of <60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 , this up-todate meta-analysis will elucidate the clinical implications of GLP-1 receptor agonists in patients with reduced eGFR.

## Chen et al. (2025): Methods


### p1

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guideline. 29 Our study is registered in the PROSPERO database, under the registration number ID: CRD42023449059.

## Chen et al. (2025): Search Strategy and Selection Criteria


### p1

We conducted a comprehensive search for published randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, the Collaboration Central Register of Controlled Clinical Trials, and the China National Knowledge Infrastructure from their inception to May 25, 2024, without language or geographic restrictions. The search terms included "GLP-1RA," "Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists," "Liraglutide," "Semaglutide," "Dulaglutide," "Albiglutide," "Lixisenatide," and "Exenatide" (Item S1). Two investigators (J-Y.C. and C-F.L.) independently conducted the search, with any disagreements resolved by a third investigator (S-Y.Y.). We then obtained the full text of the selected papers for quality assessment and data synthesis.

## Chen et al. (2025): Inclusion and Exclusion Methods


### p1

We included all RCTs that enrolled adults (≥18 years old) with varying degrees of kidney function, including individuals with CKD characterized by a baseline eGFR of <60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 . The included studies randomized their participants into experimental and control groups to compare GLP-1 receptor agonists with control treatments and assessed at least 1 of the following outcomes: (1) kidney outcomes, including acute kidney injury, the incidence of macroalbuminuria, the need for chronic kidney replacement therapy, declines in eGFR of more than 30%, 40%, and 50%, a persistent doubling of the serum creatinine level, or death from kidney disease;

### p2

(2) all-cause mortality; (3) CV outcomes such as death from CV causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization due to unstable angina. Observational studies, nonrandomized studies, letters, conference or case reports, and studies that lacked data on mortality, kidney, or CV outcomes were not included.

### p3

We conducted a thorough examination of reference lists from related studies, systematic reviews, and metaanalyses to identify further publications pertinent to our analysis. After selecting full-text articles based on quality, we conducted detailed assessments and data syntheses. Additionally, we reached out to the authors of the investigations included in our study to gather more specific information.

## Chen et al. (2025): Data Extraction


### p1

All the relevant data were extracted from the included studies by two investigators (S-Y.Y. and T-W.H.). Characteristics of each study, including the sample size in both the GLP-1 receptor agonist and control groups, population setting and site, types of GLP-1 receptor agonist, number of CKD patients in each group, respectively, and the levels of urinary albumin-creatinine ratio (UACR), were recorded. We also recorded outcome-related variables, including duration of treatment, study quality, and clinical outcomes between the 2 groups.

## Chen et al. (2025): Assessment of Risk of Bias


### p1

We utilized version 2 of the Cochrane tool for assessing the risk of bias in randomized trials (RoB 2) to assess the risk of bias in the included RCTs across 5 domains: randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported results. 30 An "overall risk of bias" for a study was judged as "low risk" if all domains were at low risk of bias, and as "high risk" if any domain showed a high risk of bias or if several domains raised concerns that significantly reduced confidence in the results. ## Chen et al. (2025): Definition of GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Users Versus Control Group


### p1

Patients in the GLP-1 receptor agonist group were treated with lixisenatide, liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide, albiglutide, efpeglenatide, or exenatide. Control group participants were treated with placebo or other preassigned treatments. Standard care with background diabetes medications, such as insulin or oral antidiabetic drugs, was maintained throughout the trials.

## Chen et al. (2025): Outcomes


### p1

The primary outcome of this meta-analysis was a composite of kidney events to capture significant disease progression across different studies. These events included acute kidney injury, new onset of macroalbuminuria, the need for chronic kidney replacement therapy, a prespecified range of eGFR decline, persistent doubling of serum creatinine levels, or death from kidney disease (with death from CV causes also included in kidney events in the FLOW study). Given the variability in end point definitions among the GLP-1 receptor agonist trials not primarily focused on kidney outcomes, we described and integrated these study-specific definitions into a composite analysis. This approach allowed us to account for the diverse definitions of kidney function deterioration while ensuring comparability across studies. To balance broader end points with rigor, we included secondary outcomes, such as all-cause mortality and declines in eGFR exceeding 30%, 40%, and 50%, only from studies that reported these outcomes using consistent definitions. Additionally, we assessed a composite of CV outcomes, encompassing the first occurrence of CV death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization for unstable angina.

## Chen et al. (2025): Data Synthesis and Statistical Analysis


### p1

We used a random-effects model to account for variations in patient baseline characteristics, study designs, and outcome definitions. This approach accommodates variations in treatment effects across studies. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs were estimated using the Mantel-Haenszel method. For between-study variance, we applied the DerSimonian-Laird method, which is widely used and suitable for meta-analyses involving a moderate number of included studies. Heterogeneity was assessed using the χ 2 test and the I 2 statistic, with P < 0.05 or I 2 >50% indicating significant heterogeneity. In cases of considerable heterogeneity, sensitivity analyses were performed by sequentially omitting glycemic control studies. Publication bias was examined using Doi plots and the Luis Furuya-Kanamori (LFK) index. The LFK index measures asymmetry, where values close to 0 indicate symmetry, and scores beyond ±1 suggest the potential presence of publication bias. 32 We assessed the quality of evidence using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) criteria. 33 All statistical analyses were conducted using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis software (version 3.3.070; Biostat) and R 4.1.1 (R Project). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

## Chen et al. (2025): Results


## Chen et al. (2025): Study Search Outcomes and Included Patients


### p1

Through a comprehensive search, we identified 212 studies. After removing 165 duplicates and excluding 33 based on eligibility criteria, 14 studies were selected for qualitative analysis (Table 1). However, we excluded the ELIXA study because lixisenatide, a short-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist with a half-life of only 2 to 3 hours, may not provide sufficient exposure with its once-daily administration design. Additionally, this study did not report kidney or CV outcomes for subgroups of patients with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 . 34 The AMPLITUDE-O trial, despite including 31.6% of patients with CKD, was excluded from our analysis because it used an eGFR cutoff of 71.5 mL/min/1.73 m 2 for subgroup analysis of the composite CV outcome, 35 conflicting with our criterion of eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 for CKD definition. Attempts to obtain the necessary data from the authors of ELIXA and AMPLITUDE-O were unsuccessful, leading to their exclusion. Ultimately, 12 studies incorporating data and outcomes of interest from 17,996 patients with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 were selected for the final meta-analysis (Fig 1). The SELECT trial was the only study that included nondiabetic patients with obesity and CV disease 36 whereas all other trials focused on patients with type 2 diabetes. All trials compared the effects of GLP-1 receptor agonist with placebo, except for AWARD-7 8 in which insulin glargine was used as the control.

### p2

All the enrolled studies were published within the 9year period from 2016 to 2024, as detailed in Table 1 and Supplementary File 2. Only the AWARD-7 and FLOW trials included more than half of their participants with a baseline UACR of 30 mg/g or higher. The proportion of participants with a baseline eGFR of <60 mL/ min/1.73 m 2 ranged from 10.8% to 100% across the selected studies (Fig S1, Supplementary File 2). Ten trials investigated kidney outcomes, 8,[19][20][21]28,[36][37][38][39][40] and all reported on all-cause mortality and CV outcomes, as illustrated in Table 2. All 12 trials were judged to have an overall low risk of bias (Fig S2).

## Chen et al. (2025): Composite Kidney Outcome and eGFR-based Outcomes


### p1

An analysis of kidney outcomes in subgroups of patients with reduced baseline eGFR from the SUSTAIN 6 is not publicly available. 19 Instead, we utilized data from a post hoc pooled analysis of participant-level data from the LEADER and SUSTAIN 6 studies. 41

### p2

The components of the composite kidney outcome varied across trials, as detailed in Table S1. In 10 trials 10,21,22,28,37,[39][40][41][42] with available data specifically for 15,164 patients with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/ 1.73 m 2 , a total of 1,915 patients experienced composite kidney outcome during the study periods. The risk of composite kidney outcome was significantly lower in the GLP-1 receptor agonist group (weighted event rate 15.1%) compared with the placebo group (17.5%) (OR, 0.85 [95% CI, 0.77-0.94], P = 0.001). The heterogeneity among included studies was low (I 2 < 0.01%) (Fig 2).

### p3

Additionally, declines in eGFR beyond specific thresholds from baseline were reported in 6 trials across 5 studies, including a pooled analysis of the SUSTAIN-6 and LEADER trials. 10,21,22,28,41 Patients treated with a GLP-1 receptor agonist exhibited a significantly lower risk of an eGFR decline greater than 30% compared with those in the placebo group (OR, 0.78 [95% CI, 0.66-0.92], P = 0.004) (Fig 3A). A similar protective effect was observed with eGFR reductions exceeding 40% (OR, 0.76 [95% CI, 0.61-0.95], P = 0.01) (Fig 3B), and even more so for declines greater than 50% (OR, 0.72 [95% CI, 0.61-0.85], P < 0.001) (Fig 3C).

## Chen et al. (2025): All-Cause Death and Composite CV Outcome


### p1

Seven studies documented the incidence of all-cause death among 11,949 patients with baseline CKD. 8,28,37,39,40,42,43 During the study periods, 1,388 patients died. The mortality risk was lower in the GLP-1 receptor agonist group (weighted event rate, 10.6%) compared with the placebo group (13.2%), with an OR of 0.77 ([95% CI, 0.60-0.98], P = 0.03). The heterogeneity among these studies was high (I 2 = 71.6%) (Fig 4A).

### p2

Of the 12 enrolled studies, 11 reported on the incidence of composite CV outcome specifically in patients with CKD at enrollment. 8,19,28,[36][37][38][39][40][44][45][46] Among the 16,368 participants, 2,121 experienced a composite CV outcome. The GLP-1 receptor agonist group showed a significant reduction in composite CV outcome (weighted event rate, 12.1%) compared with the placebo group (14.0%) (OR, 0.86 [95% CI, 0.74-0.99], P = 0.03). However, the included studies displayed moderate heterogeneity (I 2 = 40.3%) (Fig 4B).

## Chen et al. (2025): Sensitivity Analysis


### p1

It is important to recognize that kidney and CV outcomes were evaluated as safety end points in the glycemic control trials, 8,37,40 which were not designed or powered to assess the effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists on clinical kidney or CV events. After excluding LIRA-RENAL, AWARD-7, and PIONEER 5 trials for sensitivity analysis, the results for composite kidney outcome remained consistent (OR, 0.86 [95% CI, 0.77-0.95], P = 0.002) (Fig S3A). Similarly, the observed difference in composite CV outcome between the GLP-1 receptor agonist and placebo groups for remained significant after the sensitivity analysis (OR, 0.84 [95% CI, 0.72-0.99], P = 0.03) (Fig S3B). For a more refined analysis, we excluded trials that used exendin-4 backbone GLP-1 receptor agonists (lixisenatide, exenatide, and efpeglenatide) due to their distinct pharmacologic properties and potential differences in clinical efficacy. 47,48 We focused solely on those employing human GLP-1 backbone GLP-1 receptor agonists (dulaglutide, liraglutide, semaglutide, and albiglutide). This sensitivity analysis suggested that participants using these specific GLP-1 receptor agonists exhibited a lower risk of composite kidney outcome (OR, 0.83 [95% CI, 0.75-0.92], P = 0.001) (Fig 5A), all-cause mortality (OR, 0.70 [95% CI, 0.55-0.89], P = 0.003) (Fig 5B), and composite CV outcome (OR, 0.78 [95% CI, 0.70-0.87], P < 0.001) (Fig 5C) compared with those receiving placebo. Notably, the ORs for all outcomes were generally even lower than in the original analyses.

## Chen et al. (2025): Adverse Events


### p1

Adverse events were reported across trials with varying definitions, and only 5 trials provided CKD subgroupspecific data (Table S2). 8,28,37,40,42 Among CKD patients, there was no significant difference in overall adverse events between GLP-1 receptor agonists and placebo (OR, 1.23 [95% CI, 1.00-1.53], P = 0.055) (Fig S4A). However, GLP-1 receptor agonist treatment was associated with fewer serious adverse events (OR, 0.78 [95% CI, 0.66-0.92], P = 0.003) (Fig S4B) but a higher incidence of adverse events leading to trial withdrawal (OR, 1.95 [95% CI, 1.23-3.10], P = 0.005) (Fig S4C). Gastrointestinal events, such as nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, were more frequent in the GLP-1 receptor agonist group (OR, 3.18 [95% CI, 1.51-6.70], P = 0.002) (Fig S4D). There was no significant difference in acute pancreatitis, severe hypoglycemia, or nervous system disorders between the GLP-1 receptor agonists and placebo (Fig S4E-G).

## Chen et al. (2025): Publication Bias


### p1

The Doi plot for the composite kidney outcome indicated a minor asymmetry (LFK = -1.41) (Fig S5A), suggesting potential publication bias where negative or smaller-effect studies may be underrepresented. However, the Doi plots for all-cause mortality (LFK = -0.38) and CV outcomes (LFK = -0.3) exhibited symmetry, implying no significant publication bias in these outcomes (Fig S5B-C).

## Chen et al. (2025): Certainty of Evidence


### p1

The quality of evidence for the effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists on composite kidney outcomes was rated as high (≥4 points) according to GRADE criteria (Table S3). However, after excluding 2 exendin-4 backbone GLP-1 receptor agonists (EXSCEL and FREEDOM-CVO), this rating was downgraded to moderate due to inconsistency (I 2 > 40%). The evidence quality for composite CV outcome was rated as moderate but improved to high after the exclusion. For all-cause mortality, the evidence remained moderate due to heterogeneity, regardless of exclusions.

## Chen et al. (2025): Discussion


### p1

This updated meta-analysis of RCTs on GLP-1 receptor agonists, notably including the FLOW trial-the first trial dedicated to kidney outcomes within this drug class-reveals key insights. It confirms that GLP-1 receptor agonists effectively reduce the risk of adverse kidney outcomes, specifically for individuals with baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/  Currently, the most robust evidence supporting the CV and kidney benefits of GLP-1 receptor agonists derives from CVOTs. 15 However, trials like ELIXA, 34 LEADER, 44 SUSTAIN-6, 19 EXSCEL, 49 Harmony Outcomes, 45 REWIND, 50 PIONEER 6, 38 and AMPLITUDE-O 35 primarily enrolled diabetic patients with preserved eGFR, with no trials including more than a third of participants with reduced baseline eGFR (Fig S1). By contrast, glycemic control trials specifically targeting patients with CKD, such as LIRA-RENAL, 37 AWARD-7, 10 and PIONEER 5, 40 did not demonstrate significant differences in kidney outcomes, possibly due to inadequate power to detect changes in kidney outcomes. This limitation underscores the necessity of more targeted analyses, which our study addresses.

### p2

Our meta-analysis builds upon prior research by offering a focused and comprehensive evaluation of GLP-1 receptor agonists specifically in patients with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 . Although earlier studies provided valuable insights into GLP-1 receptor agonists and conducted subgroup analyses for patients with reduced eGFR, they primarily emphasized broader patient populations and offered limited analyses specific to those with reduced eGFR. 15,51 Additionally, although Mendonc ¸a et al 51 incorporated data from important recent trials like SELECT and FLOW, it did not include detailed analyses of CV outcomes or all-cause mortality. In our work, we expanded upon these foundations by incorporating data from a broader range of trials, such as LIRA-RENAL, AWARD-7, PIONEER 5, and REWIND, ensuring a more comprehensive assessment of both kidney and CV outcomes. By focusing exclusively on this high-risk CKD population and evaluating a wide range of clinical outcomes, our meta-analysis provides a more detailed and clinically meaningful understanding of GLP-1 receptor agonists' efficacy. This work complements and extends existing evidence, affirming the potential of GLP-1 receptor agonists as a therapeutic option alongside other kidneyprotective agents.

### p3

Real-world evidence from database studies supports the effectiveness of GLP-1 receptor agonists in improving kidney outcomes, mirroring controlled trial results. In the United States, military veterans using GLP-1 receptor agonists experienced a 28% lower risk of adverse kidney outcomes compared with those taking sulfonylureas, and a 21% lower risk compared with those taking dipeptidyl   peptidase 4 (DPP-4) inhibitors. 52 In northern Europe, Scandinavian and Swedish cohorts showed that GLP-1 receptor agonists reduced the risk of composite kidney outcomes by 24% and 28%, respectively, when compared with DPP-4 inhibitors. 53,54 Furthermore, a study in Taiwan targeting diabetic patients with a baseline eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m 2 demonstrated that GLP-1 receptor agonists significantly reduced composite kidney events compared with DPP-4 inhibitors (subdistribution HR, 0.72 [95% CI, 0.56-0.93]), 55 underscoring their efficacy beyond controlled trials. The SELECT trial, the only large GLP-1 receptor agonist study enrolling nondiabetic patients with obesity and CV disease, is also included in our meta-analysis by incorporating kidney outcomes and mortality data from Colhoun et al. 42 This trial offers unique insights into the GLP-1 receptor agonist effects in a nondiabetic population. Because GLP-1 receptor agonists are increasingly used for weight reduction in nondiabetic individuals, upcoming studies will likely provide further evidence. Future metaanalyses may help clarify differences in GLP-1 receptor agonist effects between diabetic and nondiabetic patients. Additionally, trials focused primarily on kidney outcomes in nondiabetic CKD populations are necessary for more comprehensive insights into GLP-1 receptor agonists' impact on kidney function in these patients.

### p4

Previous studies have indicated that the kidney protective effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists are primarily attributed to their ability to reduce or prevent macroalbuminuria. 15,56 However, although albuminuria is closely associated with clinical kidney outcomes, 57 it is considered a surrogate end point and is less crucial than direct outcomes such as the rate of eGFR decline or ESKD. Our meta-analysis, which employed end points of sustained eGFR declines of at least 30%, 40%, or 50%, demonstrates that GLP-1 receptor agonists protect against eGFR-based kidney outcomes in patients with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 (Fig 3). Grams et al 58 demonstrated that an intervention reducing the eGFR decline by 0.75 mL/min/1.73 m 2 per year was significantly associated with a lower risk of ESKD. Dulaglutide, semaglutide, and liraglutide can either exceed or approach this treatment threshold in patients with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 as seen in the AWARD-7, SUSTAIN 6, LEADER, and PIONEER 6 trials. 8,22,41 Notably, a more significant slowing in the rate of eGFR decline was observed among participants with pre-existing impaired eGFR compared with those with preserved eGFR in these RTCs (Table S4). 22,23,41 In the FLOW and SELECT trials, the treatment difference in eGFR slope was significantly in favor of semaglutide compared with placebo in patients with baseline CKD, with a mean difference higher than 1 mL/min/1.73 m 2 per year. 28,42 These findings reinforce the conclusion that GLP-1 receptor agonists not only prevent macroalbuminuria but also slow the decline in eGFR in patients with CKD.

### p5

Exploratory mediation analysis suggests that the kidney benefits of GLP-1 receptor agonists are only partially attributed to weight reduction, hemoglobin A 1c , and blood pressure control. 59 The underlying mechanisms are not yet fully understood. [24][25][26] It may involve inhibiting the Na + / H + exchanger 3 in the proximal tubules, influencing the afferent arteriolar smooth muscle cells, and promoting natriuresis and tubuloglomerular feedback. 27 It remains uncertain whether these mechanisms persist as renal tubular and vascular function declines. Additionally, antiinflammatory and antioxidant effects may contribute to its kidney protection. 24,60 Further clinical studies, such as the ongoing REMODEL trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04865770), 61 are necessary to confirm these effects in diabetes and CKD.

### p6

Research has shown that various GLP-1 receptor agonists significantly reduce the risk of CV events in diabetic patients with established cardiovascular disease or high CV risk. 19,35,44,45,50 Notably, dulaglutide, liraglutide, and subcutaneous semaglutide can be administered to patients with advanced CKD without dose adjustments, 62 making them particularly suitable for those with reduced eGFR and elevated CV risk. Although the impact of GLP-1 receptor agonists on CV outcomes remains uncertain in participants with a baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 , as indicated by previous meta-analysis and post hoc pooled analyses, 15,18 our current meta-analysis of 16,368 participants from 11 rigorously conducted RCTs has demonstrated its beneficial CV effects specifically in patients with CKD (Fig 4B). This study provides compelling evidence of the CV protective potential of GLP-1 receptor agonists in the CKD population, suggesting a significant role in shaping clinical strategies for patients with compromised kidney function.

### p7

Our sensitivity analyses reveal that excluding trials utilizing the exendin-4 backbone agents from this metaanalysis appears to enhance the beneficial effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists (Fig 5). This finding is consistent with those from a retrospective observational study in Italy, which indicated that human GLP-1 backbone GLP-1 receptor agonists confer greater CV benefits compared with those with an exendin-4 backbone. 48 Indeed, only trials employing human GLP-1 backbone reported eGFRbased kidney outcomes specifically for participants with a reduced baseline eGFR (Fig 3). Another possibility is that the findings were influenced by poor adherence in EXSCEL trial, where over 40% of participants discontinued treatment prematurely and permanently. 49 Furthermore, exenatide, the GLP-1 receptor agonist used in the EXSCEL and FREEDOM-CVO trials, is exclusively eliminated through the kidneys, 47 with significantly reduced clearance in patients with kidney dysfunction. 63 Although infrequent, there have been case reports of acute kidney failure associated with exenatide shortly after GLP-1 receptor agonists were introduced. 64 These observations imply a correlation between the molecular structure of GLP-1 receptor agonists and their clinical effects, providing hints for further research but not a direct comparison of superiority.

### p8

Our analysis highlights key safety considerations for using GLP-1 receptor agonists in CKD patients. While the overall risk of adverse events was similar between the GLP-1 receptor agonists and placebo, GLP-1 receptor agonist treatment was associated with a higher incidence of adverse events leading to trial withdrawal and more frequent gastrointestinal disorders. These gastrointestinal events are typically mild to moderate, and there is no evidence to suggest an increased risk of acute pancreatitis. These findings are consistent with observations from the LEADER and REWIND trials, which included predominantly patients with preserved eGFR (75%-80%). 44,50 Notably, GLP-1 receptor agonist treatment was associated with fewer serious adverse events, suggesting potential benefits despite these tolerability concerns. It is important to note that only 5 trials provided CKD subgroup-specific adverse event data, which may introduce bias into these analyses. Careful monitoring remains essential to manage adverse effects effectively in high-risk CKD patients.

### p9

We acknowledge certain limitations beyond those inherent in meta-analysis. First, kidney outcomes were not the primary outcome in most RCTs, leading to variability in in outcome definitions across studies (Table S1). Although this variability may influence our findings, the selected components are clinically relevant and aligned with disease progression, consistent with recent meta-analyses of kidney outcomes. 65,66 To address this, we conducted sensitivity analyses excluding glycemic control trials and performed separate analyses using consistent definitions of eGFR decline. Second, there was variability in the CKD patient populations across the included studies, including differences in CKD stage and the presence of albuminuria, leading to increased heterogeneity in the meta-analysis. The wide confidence intervals for some I 2 estimates (Table S5) suggest that heterogeneity may be underestimated, indicating the need for cautious interpretation. Additionally, some studies lacked complete data; despite attempts to contact the authors, we could only obtain excerpts from post hoc or subgroup analyses rather than patient-level data from the original RCTs, which could have affected the accuracy of the overall results. Furthermore, although our study primarily focused on CKD patients, except for the SELECT trial, 36 all others were conducted in diabetic populations, necessitating further observation to ensure generalizability to all CKD patients.

### p10

We also acknowledge the potential instability of the DerSimonian-Laird method in analyses with small study numbers. 67 However, this issue is less relevant to our overall analyses, which included up to 11 studies. We considered alternatives like Bayesian approaches, but they were not used because of concerns about bias from prior assumptions. 68 For outcomes such as eGFR declines, the small number of included studies may introduce variability and increase uncertainty in the estimates. Doi plots suggest potential underreporting of small studies with negative results for composite kidney outcome, possibly influencing the pooled estimate. Although the asymmetry is minor, it still highlights the need for caution when interpreting the findings.

### p11

In conclusion, our meta-analysis shows strong evidence that GLP-1 receptor agonists offer kidney benefits for patients with reduced eGFR. Human GLP-1 backbone GLP-1 receptor agonists may reduce the incidence of eGFR decline, all-cause death, and composite CV events in CKD patients. This highlights their essential role in CKD management. Because the mechanisms underlying GLP-1 receptor agonist's kidney protections are not clear, this represents an opportunity for bedside-to-bench research to explore kidney pathology.
