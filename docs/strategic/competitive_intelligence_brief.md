# Competitive Intelligence Brief
Strategic reference. The GLP-1 obesity and Type 2 diabetes competitive landscape Rabivy is entering, as of early 2026.

## Market Structure
The branded GLP-1 obesity market is a two-player contest between Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly, with Novo historically dominant and Lilly gaining rapidly. Novo Nordisk led the global GLP-1 obesity market in 2025 with roughly a 59.6% branded volume share, while Lilly's Zepbound recorded about $13.5B in full-year revenue on a ~174% US increase - signalling fast competitive erosion of Novo's lead. Novo's global diabetes value share fell about 3.6 points to ~30.1% over the same period.

## The Competitors

### Novo Nordisk - Ozempic (semaglutide, Type 2 diabetes)
Weekly injection. ~$17.8B 2025 global sales. The anchor diabetes brand; widely prescribed and frequently used off-label historically.

### Novo Nordisk - Wegovy (semaglutide, obesity)
Weekly injection. ~$11.1B 2025 global sales. The obesity-indicated sibling to Ozempic. Strong commercial formulary access achieved within months of launch.

### Eli Lilly - Mounjaro (tirzepatide, Type 2 diabetes)
Weekly injection. ~$22.97B 2025 global sales. Dual GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist. The single largest brand by revenue in the set.

### Eli Lilly - Zepbound (tirzepatide, obesity)
Weekly injection. ~$13.54B 2025 global sales. Fast launch trajectory, reaching high US market share within months and a large weekly prescription base early.

## Mechanism Comparison
- Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy): GLP-1 receptor agonist.
- Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound): GLP-1 and GIP receptor *agonist* (dual agonist).
- Rabivy (trelaglutide): GLP-1 receptor agonism combined with GIP receptor *antagonism*, delivered via an antibody conjugate. Distinct from tirzepatide's dual-agonist approach.

## Where Rabivy Wins
- Dosing: monthly (or less frequent) vs weekly for every competitor. The clearest, most defensible differentiator.
- Durability: no weight-loss plateau observed at 52 weeks in Phase 2, versus the plateau pattern associated with existing agents.
- Adherence/persistence: fewer injections directly targets the discontinuation problem that erodes competitor patient bases over time.

## Where Rabivy Is Behind
- Approval and evidence: competitors are approved with large real-world evidence bases and years of prescriber familiarity; Rabivy is investigational with Phase 2 data only.
- Entrenchment: Novo and Lilly hold established formulary positions and prescriber habits. Switching requires a specific, patient-level reason.
- Scale: competitors have very large installed prescriber and patient bases at launch.

## Commercial Takeaway
Do not fight on raw efficacy alone against entrenched, approved agents. Compete on injection burden and durability, target the plateaued and adherence-challenged patient subgroups, and prioritise switchers on Novo- or Lilly-heavy panels where a convenience argument lands.
