# Payer & Market Access Brief
Strategic reference. GLP-1 coverage landscape relevant to Rabivy launch planning, as of early 2026.

## Why Access Is the Bottleneck
For GLP-1 obesity therapies, the clinical decision to prescribe is only half the battle. Coverage, prior authorization, and step therapy determine whether the patient actually starts and stays on treatment. Access requirements vary sharply by payer type, and this variation is the single biggest driver of the gap between a high-propensity prescriber and realised Rabivy volume.

## Coverage by Payer Type

### Commercial (employer & private plans)
Strongest near-term opportunity. Largest insured population. Diabetes indication is well covered; obesity coverage is growing but varies by employer, with larger employers more likely to cover. Prior authorization is required by most major carriers, typically needing documentation of BMI or comorbidities. Step therapy (fail cheaper alternatives or lifestyle first) is common, especially for the obesity indication.

### Medicare Part D (federal, 65+)
Moderate opportunity, improving. Diabetes coverage is well established. Obesity coverage has historically been restricted but is expanding. Prior authorization is effectively universal - providers must attest BMI, comorbidities, and prior weight-management attempts. PA burden and plan-level variation limit real-world conversion even where coverage exists.

### Medicaid (state/federal, low-income)
Highest barriers across every dimension. Obesity coverage of GLP-1s is optional and state-dependent, and the number of states covering it has been narrowing - roughly 13 states as of January 2026, down from about 16 in 2025. Where coverage exists, PA requirements are high and there is no uniform national standard. State-by-state fragmentation makes access highly unpredictable. Treat Medicaid-heavy panels as low near-term conversion regardless of prescriber propensity.

### Cash Pay (out-of-pocket)
No formulary applies; any approved GLP-1 is accessible if the patient can afford it. List prices in the ~$900-$1,300/month range limit this to a small, higher-income segment. No PA, no step therapy.

## The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Demonstration (July 2026)
A time-limited Medicare demonstration program is set to begin in July 2026, temporarily opening limited obesity-indication coverage for GLP-1s under defined conditions. This is a moving target and could shift the Medicare opportunity between planning and Rabivy launch. Payer mix and formulary assumptions should be revisited close to launch rather than fixed now.

## Implication for Targeting
Access-adjust the propensity score. A high raw propensity HCP with a Medicaid-heavy, not-covered panel and high PA burden is a lower realistic conversion than the raw score implies. Commercial-heavy panels with preferred or non-preferred formulary status convert most readily. Use the formulary status and PA burden fields in each HCP snapshot to triage where rep effort will actually produce fills.
