# GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Class Overview
Clinical reference. Background on the GLP-1 class Rabivy is entering. For internal use.

## What GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Do
Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists mimic the incretin hormone GLP-1. They enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying, and act on central appetite pathways to reduce food intake. The result is improved glycemic control in Type 2 diabetes and meaningful weight loss in obesity. Incretin therapy has become the backbone of pharmacological obesity treatment.

## Incretin Mechanisms in the Class
- GLP-1 receptor agonism: the core mechanism (semaglutide).
- GLP-1 + GIP dual agonism: adds glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptor agonism (tirzepatide).
- GLP-1 agonism + GIP antagonism: Rabivy's approach, combining GLP-1 receptor agonism with GIP receptor antagonism.
The role of GIP - and whether agonism or antagonism is preferable alongside GLP-1 - is an area of active scientific interest, and Rabivy sits on the antagonism side of that question.

## Approved Indications
The class is approved across two principal indications: Type 2 diabetes (glycemic control) and chronic weight management (obesity/overweight with comorbidity). Individual brands are approved for one or both. Cardiovascular and other outcome indications have expanded for some agents.

## Landmark Trials
- STEP program (semaglutide, obesity): STEP 1 showed ~14.9% mean weight loss at 68 weeks with once-weekly semaglutide 2.4mg in adults with overweight or obesity.
- SURMOUNT program (tirzepatide, obesity): SURMOUNT-1 showed up to ~22.5% mean weight loss at 72 weeks.
- SUSTAIN / SURPASS programs established the diabetes efficacy of semaglutide and tirzepatide respectively.
These trials set the efficacy and durability benchmarks against which new entrants are evaluated.

## Why Dosing Frequency Matters Clinically
All currently approved agents in this space are weekly subcutaneous injections. Real-world persistence is a known weakness of the class: a substantial share of patients discontinue within the first year, with injection burden, side effects, and cost among the drivers. Because the clinical benefit depends on staying on therapy, anything that improves persistence - such as less-frequent dosing - has outsized real-world impact relative to marginal efficacy differences. This is the clinical rationale behind Rabivy's monthly dosing.

## Class-Wide Tolerability
Gastrointestinal adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation) are the dominant tolerability issue across all agents and the leading adverse-event reason for discontinuation. Gradual dose escalation is the standard mitigation.
