# Real-World Evidence Brief: GLP-1 Persistence & Discontinuation

Clinical reference. What happens to GLP-1 patients after the prescription, in practice rather than in trials. For internal use.

## Why This Matters

Trial efficacy assumes patients stay on therapy. In the real world, many do not. The gap between trial weight loss and real-world outcomes is largely a persistence problem, and persistence is precisely where a monthly agent has a structural advantage. Reps should ground durability conversations in these realities.

## Persistence at 1 Year

Persistence has been improving but remains a challenge. Recent real-world analysis found roughly 63% of patients who started Wegovy in 2024 were still on therapy one year later - a meaningful improvement over earlier cohorts, but still meaning more than a third had stopped. Over a three-year horizon, retention drops sharply: only about one in seven Wegovy patients remained on the drug across three years in one analysis.

## Time to First Fill

Approval does not guarantee a fill. Fill rates differ markedly by indication: roughly 74% of anti-diabetic GLP-1 prescriptions were filled within 60 days, versus only about 47% of anti-obesity GLP-1 prescriptions. The obesity indication faces more access friction between script and pickup - consistent with higher PA burden and weaker obesity coverage.

## Why Patients Discontinue

Real-world discontinuation is driven primarily by side effects and cost. In one analysis, side effects accounted for about 28.2% of discontinuations and cost about 12.8%. Adherence and injection burden compound these. For obesity patients specifically, discontinuation tends to be higher and earlier than for diabetes patients.

## Prior Authorization Reality

Initial approval rates for anti-obesity GLP-1s (Wegovy, Zepbound) have at times been as low as 31-41%, meaning a large share of first requests are denied or delayed. PA support and documentation quality materially affect whether a high-propensity prescriber's scripts convert to fills.

## Commercial Implication

The persistence and fill data are the evidentiary backbone for the monthly-dosing message. The core argument: the category's biggest real-world failure is keeping patients on therapy, and reducing injection frequency directly targets one of the top drivers of discontinuation. Pair this brief with the product benefits brief when the durability or adherence objection comes up.
